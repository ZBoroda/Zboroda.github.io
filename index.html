Research

How many mutations does it take to evolve a new function? With Nida Farheen, Prof. Ethan Levien, and Prof. Jané Kondev · Brandeis University
2021 – 2025

A question that sounds simple but is surprisingly hard to answer. When a biological network evolves a new function, how many mutations does that actually take?

We modeled this using Boolean networks (think logic gates, where the wiring is the genotype and the function it computes is the phenotype). We found that the number of mutations needed scales linearly with the size of the network, and that this is driven by a surprisingly clean geometric property: what fraction of the network's connections are actually doing functional work.

I built the simulation framework in Python and ran large-scale experiments on an HPC cluster. The paper is in preparation.
